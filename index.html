Reading — Sleep and Learning

本文

Many students believe that the best way to prepare for an exam is to study as long as possible, even if it means sacrificing sleep. However, a growing body of research suggests that (1)this strategy may actually work against them. (2)Sleep, far from being a waste of time, plays a crucial role in how the brain stores and organizes new information.

When we learn something new, the brain first records it in a temporary form. It is during sleep, particularly deep sleep, that these fresh memories are gradually transferred to more permanent storage. In one well-known experiment, participants who slept after learning a list of words remembered far more of them the next day than those who stayed awake. The difference was not small; the sleepers recalled nearly twice as many items.

Sleep also helps the brain make connections between pieces of information. People often report waking up with a solution to a problem that seemed impossible the night before. This is not merely luck. While we sleep, the brain continues to work quietly, linking new knowledge to what we already know.

For students, the lesson is clear. (3)Staying up all night before a test may allow you to review more material, but it can prevent your brain from doing the very thing that makes learning last. A good night's sleep is not the opposite of hard work; it is (　A　) of it.
